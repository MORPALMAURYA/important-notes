01
The ₹499 problem
One store, two products at the same price. Two payments arrive. Which is which?

Before we touch Razorpay, let's feel the problem it exists to solve. You've built Kapda Co., a small clothing store. It sells two products, both priced at ₹499: an Oversized Black Tee and a Canvas Tote Bag. To keep things simple, you've put your UPI QR code on the checkout page. Free, instant, everyone has UPI.

One order came in this morning, and you matched it by hand. Then, between 7:41 and 7:44 PM, three more arrive. Your phone buzzes twice: ₹499 credited. ₹499 credited.

Now answer three questions — not with your gut, but the way your server would have to answer them at 3 AM with nobody awake:

  • Which ₹499 is for the tee, and which is for the tote?
  • Who do you ship to — and what do you ship them?
  • Kabir just sent a payment screenshot on WhatsApp. Did he actually pay?
Kapda Co. · 24 Sep 2026, 7:45 PM

Your store's admin panel

Your bank statement

Key Concept

Reconciliation is matching every rupee that lands in your account to the exact order it belongs to. Amount can't do it — two ₹499 products look identical in a bank statement. Payer name can't do it — people pay from a parent's or friend's account. You need something unique that is created before the payment and travels with it.

Hold on to that last sentence. Everything else in these notes is that one idea, built properly.

02
Why not just a UPI QR?
It's free and everyone has UPI. So what's missing?

At a chai tapri, a UPI QR works perfectly. You scan, pay ₹20, the soundbox announces the payment, and bhaiya hands you the chai. The verification is a human standing at the counter, listening.

Your website has no one at the counter. Your server has to decide — automatically, for every single order — whether to mark it paid and ship it. So let's look at what a QR actually gives your server.

What's inside a static QR

A UPI QR is just a link, drawn as an image. Decode one and you get this:

Decoded QR
upi://pay?pa=kapdaco@okaxis&pn=Kapda%20Co&cu=INR
ParamValue usedWhat it does
pakapdaco@okaxisPayee address — your UPI ID. Tells the money where to go.
pnKapda CoPayee name shown in the customer's UPI app.
cuINRCurrency.
am— missingAmount. Usually empty in a static QR, so the customer types it — ₹49, ₹499, anything.
tn— missingTransaction note. Optional free text.

Notice what's not there: no order, no product, no customer — and most importantly, no address for your server. The QR tells money where to go. It never tells your server that it arrived.

Where it breaks for a website

💡

"But I can pre-fill the amount and a note in the QR." You can — a dynamic QR with am=499&tn=ORDER1042 fixes the input side. But still nothing calls your server when the money lands, so you're still reading bank statements. That confirmation channel is exactly what gateways sell; even Razorpay's own UPI QR codes come with APIs and webhooks behind them.

Key Concept

A payment gateway is not a new way to move money — UPI, cards and netbanking still do that. It's the layer that ties every payment to your order and reports it to your server in a way that can't be faked.

03
Order first, pay second
Give every purchase a unique ID before a single rupee moves.

Think of a busy food court counter. Two people order the same ₹180 thali. The counter doesn't remember faces — it hands out token #47 and token #48. When the kitchen calls "47", nobody is confused, even though both thalis cost exactly the same.

A payment gateway does the same thing with an order_id. Before the customer pays, your backend asks Razorpay to create an order, and Razorpay hands back a unique token like order_PxT8Kd2mVbQ1aZ. You save it next to the product and the customer. From then on, every payment that comes back is tagged with its token.

Aarav clicks Buy on the Tee  → order_PxT7Qm4nHs2cXe → saved as pending (Tee, Aarav, ₹499)
Meera clicks Buy on the Tote → order_PxT8Kd2mVbQ1aZ → saved as pending (Tote, Meera, ₹499)
    ↓
A ₹499 payment arrives tagged order_PxT8Kd2mVbQ1aZ
    ↓
DB lookup → Tote, Meera → mark completed, ship the tote ✅

Two ₹499 payments are now as different as token 47 and token 48. That's the answer to the two-products-same-price problem: you never match by amount again.

The three IDs you'll meet

IDExampleCreated by, and whenAnswers
order_idorder_PxT8Kd2mVbQ1aZYour backend, via Razorpay — before paymentWhich purchase is this?
payment_idpay_PxTB3nLq7Rw9YeRazorpay — when the customer actually paysWhich money movement is this?

💡

One order can have several payment attempts — a declined card, then a successful UPI payment. Each attempt gets its own payment_id, but they all belong to the same order_id. That's why your database record is keyed on the order.

⚠️

Razorpay amounts are in paise. To charge ₹499 you send amount: 49900. Send 499 and the customer pays ₹4.99.

04
Who's who
Four actors, one rule: the browser asks, the backend decides.

The reference diagram has four columns. Before following the arrows, know what each one holds and how far you trust it.

ActorWhat it holdsTrust it?
Frontend (Browser)The UI, Razorpay's public key_id, whatever the user typesNo. The user controls it — DevTools can edit any request.
Backend (Express)key_secret, product prices, the verification logicYes. Every decision is made here.
MongoDBOne Payment record per order: order → product → user → statusYes. The source of truth for "is this order paid?"
RazorpayThe money movement, order_ids, payment_ids, signaturesYes — once the signature proves it's really Razorpay talking.
Key Concept

Razorpay gives you two keys. key_id (rzp_test_…) is public — it only says which merchant account a checkout belongs to, so it can go to the browser. key_secret is the password to your merchant account and the ingredient of every signature. It lives only in your backend's .env.

⚠️

Never let the frontend send the price. If your API accepts { amount: 499 } from the browser, anyone can change it to 1 and buy the tote for ₹1. The frontend says which product; the backend looks up how much.

Step 2 — The Payment model

      
One document per order. It starts as pending and later flips to completed — we update it, we never insert a second one. We also store product and user, which the reference diagram leaves out. Those two fields are what answer "who paid for what".

      

        
src/models/payment.model.js

        
const mongoose = require("mongoose");

const paymentSchema = new mongoose.Schema(
  {
    orderId:   { type: String, required: true, unique: true },
    paymentId: { type: String },
    signature: { type: String },
    amount:    { type: Number, required: true },   // in paise
    currency:  { type: String, default: "INR" },
    product:   { type: mongoose.Schema.Types.ObjectId, ref: "product", required: true },
    user:      { type: mongoose.Schema.Types.ObjectId, ref: "user", required: true },
    status:    { type: String, enum: ["pending", "completed", "failed"], default: "pending" },
  },
  { timestamps: true }
);

module.exports = mongoose.model("payment", paymentSchema);

      


      
Step 3 — The create-order controller

      

        
src/controllers/payment.controller.js

        
const razorpay = require("../config/razorpay");
const productModel = require("../models/product.model");
const paymentModel = require("../models/payment.model");

// POST /create/order/:productId   → steps 1–5
async function createOrder(req, res) {
  // Price comes from OUR database — never from req.body
  const product = await productModel.findById(req.params.productId);
  if (!product) return res.status(404).json({ message: "Product not found" });

  // Step 2–3: ask Razorpay for an order. Amount is in paise.
  const order = await razorpay.orders.create({
    amount: Math.round(product.price * 100),
    currency: "INR",
    receipt: `rcpt_${Date.now()}`,
    notes: { productId: String(product._id) },
  });

  // Step 4: a pending record that ties order → product → user
  await paymentModel.create({
    orderId: order.id,
    amount: order.amount,
    currency: order.currency,
    product: product._id,
    user: req.user._id,
    status: "pending",
  });

  // Step 5: send the browser only what checkout needs
  res.status(201).json({
    orderId: order.id,
    amount: order.amount,
    currency: order.currency,
    keyId: process.env.RAZORPAY_KEY_ID,
  });
}

Step 2 — Create the order, open checkout, forward the result

      

        
checkout.js (frontend)

        
async function buy(productId) {
  // Steps 1–5: ask OUR backend to create the order
  const { data } = await axios.post(`/create/order/${productId}`);

  // Step 6: open Razorpay Checkout for that order
  const rzp = new window.Razorpay({
    key: data.keyId,
    order_id: data.orderId,
    amount: data.amount,
    currency: data.currency,
    name: "Kapda Co.",
    description: "Canvas Tote Bag",

    // Step 9: Razorpay calls this with the three values
    handler: async function (response) {
      // Step 10: hand them to OUR backend. The browser decides nothing.
      const verify = await axios.post("/api/payment/verify", response);
      if (verify.data.status === "success") showOrderConfirmed();
      else showPaymentFailed();
    },
  });

  rzp.on("payment.failed", function (res) {
    showPaymentFailed(res.error.description);
  });

  rzp.open();
}

08

        

          
Verify the signature

          
Steps 10–14 — the screenshot that can't be faked.

        

      


      
Go back to Kabir's WhatsApp screenshot. The problem wasn't the picture — it was that anyone can make that picture. Step 9 has the same problem: the payment response reaches your backend through the browser, and anyone can open DevTools and send /api/payment/verify whatever values they like.

      
The signature fixes this. After a successful payment, Razorpay computes:

      

        
How Razorpay signs a payment

        
razorpay_signature = HMAC_SHA256(order_id + "|" + razorpay_payment_id, key_secret)

      

      
HMAC is a hash that needs a secret key. Change one character of the message, or use the wrong key, and the output is completely different. Only two parties know key_secret: Razorpay and your backend. So your backend recomputes the same HMAC and compares. If they match, only Razorpay could have produced it — and it's bound to this order and this payment.

Step 1 — The verify controller

      

        
src/controllers/payment.controller.js

        
const crypto = require("crypto");

// POST /api/payment/verify   → steps 10–14
async function verifyPayment(req, res) {
  const { razorpay_order_id, razorpay_payment_id, razorpay_signature } = req.body;

  // Step 11: recompute what Razorpay would have signed
  const expected = crypto
    .createHmac("sha256", process.env.RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET)
    .update(`${razorpay_order_id}|${razorpay_payment_id}`)
    .digest("hex");

  // Step 14: no match → someone tampered with or invented these values
  if (expected !== razorpay_signature) {
    return res.status(400).json({ status: "failure", message: "Invalid signature" });
  }

  // Step 12: update the SAME record we saved as pending in step 4
  const payment = await paymentModel.findOneAndUpdate(
    { orderId: razorpay_order_id },
    { paymentId: razorpay_payment_id, signature: razorpay_signature, status: "completed" },
    { new: true }
  );
  if (!payment) return res.status(404).json({ status: "failure", message: "Order not found" });

  // Step 13
  res.json({ status: "success", orderId: payment.orderId });
}

      

      

        
💡

        
The Razorpay SDK ships the same check as a helper: validatePaymentVerification({ order_id, payment_id }, signature, secret) from razorpay/dist/utils/razorpay-utils. Write it by hand once so you know there's no magic — it's the five lines above.

09

        

          
When things go wrong

          
Closed tabs, repeated requests, and the mistakes that cost real money.

        

      


      
The closed-tab problem

      
Meera pays. Her UPI app confirms. But before Razorpay's popup hands control back, her phone dies. The handler never runs, so step 10 never happens. Razorpay has her money; your database still says pending. She'll be emailing you by morning.

      

        Payment captured at Razorpay ✅

            ↓

        Browser closes before the handler runs ✗

            ↓

        /api/payment/verify is never called

            ↓

        DB stuck at pending — paid customer, unshipped order ✗
      

      
The fix is a webhook: you give Razorpay a URL on your backend, and Razorpay calls it directly — server to server — whenever a payment event happens. No browser in the middle, so nothing can close.


      
Step 1 — The webhook route

      

        
app.js + webhook controller

        
// Register BEFORE app.use(express.json()) — the signature is over the RAW body
app.post("/api/payment/webhook", express.raw({ type: "application/json" }), handleWebhook);
app.use(express.json());

async function handleWebhook(req, res) {
  const expected = crypto
    .createHmac("sha256", process.env.RAZORPAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET)
    .update(req.body)                        // raw Buffer, not parsed JSON
    .digest("hex");

  if (expected !== req.headers["x-razorpay-signature"]) return res.status(400).end();

  const event = JSON.parse(req.body);
  if (event.event === "payment.captured") {
    const p = event.payload.payment.entity;
    await paymentModel.findOneAndUpdate(
      { orderId: p.order_id, status: "pending" },
      { paymentId: p.id, status: "completed" }
    );
  }

  res.status(200).json({ received: true });  // 2xx, or Razorpay retries
}

      

      

        
💡

        
The webhook secret is a different secret from key_secret — you choose it when you add the webhook in the Razorpay dashboard. Webhooks need a public URL, so for local development expose your server with a tunnel such as ngrok.

      

      
Now the same record can be completed by either path — the browser's verify call or the webhook — whichever arrives first. That's why both updates target the record by orderId, and why anything with side effects (reducing stock, sending the confirmation email) should run only when the status actually changes from pending.

Recap: why, then what

          
The whole module in five questions.

        

      


      

        

          
Why…Because…

          
            
…not a UPI QR on the website?Nothing tells your server a payment arrived, and nothing links it to an order. Amount and payer name can't tell two ₹499 products apart.

            
…create the order on the backend first?It gives each purchase a unique order_id — the food-court token — and locks the amount to the price in your DB.

            
…save it as pending before payment?So order_id → product → user exists before any money moves. Later you update that same record.

            
…verify the signature?The payment response came through the browser. Only an HMAC made with key_secret proves Razorpay sent it, for this exact order and payment.

            
…add a webhook?The browser might never come back. Razorpay can always reach your server directly.

          
        

      


      

        
Key Concept

        
A payment gateway turns "₹499 credited" into "Meera paid ₹499 for the Canvas Tote Bag, order_PxT8Kd2mVbQ1aZ, and here's proof". Everything you built — the order, the pending record, the signature, the webhook — exists to make that sentence true without a human checking.
